# Demo script

Narration for the recorded silent cut (2:37 of screen footage from the production site, `odyssey-blush-seven.vercel.app`). It is timed so each part fits its take at a calm pace, about 140 words a minute. Say it in your own words if that sounds more natural, but keep the facts, and keep "demo data" wherever the screen is a mockup.

The narration must be a team member's own voice: ETHGlobal rejects AI or text-to-speech voiceover.

**How to record:** play `silent-cut.mp4` and read along in QuickTime (*File → New Audio Recording*), or record each take as its own file (`voice-01.m4a` … `voice-08.m4a`). Then the voice gets mixed onto the footage and the cut is stretched or trimmed to fit it.

**Optional clips from the team** make it stronger, if there's time:
- **2b:** 8–10 s of the real glasses capturing (filmed with a laptop webcam or camera, not a phone), cut in after take 2.
- **6b:** 20–30 s of Claude answering from the dataset over MCP, cut in after take 6. Needs a lab wallet that subscribed and its MCP token.

---

## 1 · Hook (0:00–0:13) · landing page, the glasses come apart in the Lineup view

> AI is running out of internet. Robots need real, first-person data, but labs can't tell real data from fakes, and the people who capture it never get paid. This is Odyssey.

## 2 · The capture device (0:13–0:32) · `/device`, the 3D glasses running the capture cycle

> These are our capture glasses: an ESP32 camera with a key that never leaves the device. When you capture, the glasses sign the clip right at the sensor, then upload it. Ready, capturing, signing, uploading, verified.

## 3 · One human, one device (0:32–0:49) · `/mockups/enroll`, demo data

> Each pair of glasses belongs to one real person. You enter the glasses' address and prove you're human with World ID, without revealing who you are. The proof only works for that device, so one human gets one device.

## 4 · Proof on Arkiv (0:49–1:13) · demo dataset sample proof, then a real sample on the Arkiv explorer

> Every sample carries its proof: the device, a content hash and the device's signature. And this one isn't a mockup. It's a real photo from our glasses, stored as an entity on Arkiv: the device address, the World ID that captured it, the location and the signature, all public. The photo itself stays encrypted.

## 5 · A lab pays in 0G (1:13–1:44) · demo subscribe, the hex coverage map, then the real contract on 0G Galileo

> An AI lab picks an area and subscribes for thirty days, paid in 0G. Once subscribed, it gets a live map of every hexagon its data covers, and how validators scored each one. Payments go through our subscriptions contract, deployed on 0G Galileo. Here it is, with a real subscription payment. Seventy percent of every payment is set aside for the people who captured the data.

## 6 · Ask an AI about the data (1:44–1:54) · docs, *Use it from an LLM (MCP)*

> Labs can also search the data they paid for straight from Claude or any MCP client, through our hosted MCP server.

## 7 · Cheaters get caught (1:54–2:20) · docs, *Two strikes and you're out*, then ConsensusRegistry on 0G Galileo

> Faking data has a price. If someone re-uploads another person's clip, that's provable, so it's a strike. Two strikes, and their World ID is banned: no uploads, no new devices, and their data can't be decrypted. Bans are recorded on our consensus contract on 0G, here with the validator votes and test bans we ran.

## 8 · Contributors get paid (2:20–2:37) · `/mockups/analytics`, demo data, then the deployed contracts

> Contributors see what they've earned each reward epoch and claim it from the contract, while the platform view tracks revenue and the contributor share. Real-world data: signed at the sensor, checked by validators, paid to the people who capture it. That's Odyssey.
